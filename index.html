Threat Focus

Social engineering

Social engineering is a common tactic used by attackers to take advantage of the human element and manipulate others by leveraging trust, urgency and authority to gain access to sensitive information or restricted areas.

It's natural for us to want to be helpful especially when talking to someone that seems to be in need or distress, but its important to exercise caution.

Because social engineering can be used to bypass even the most modern technical defenses, it is considered an evolving threat.

How social engineering works

Phishing is one of the most common forms of social engineering and its designed around coercing people into giving up sensitive information or installing viruses onto your computer such as ransomware.

Phishing as a common example

An example of a phishing attempt would be receiving an email that appears to be legitimate from somewhere like a bank asking you to click on a link to secure our account due to an “unauthorized purchase”. In reality there is no unauthorized charge on your account and the attacker will use that link to either install malware on your system or harvest your credentials by creating a fake webpage that looks somewhat similar to the one you might be used to using for your bank and wait for you to enter your login credentials.

Warning signs

Thankfully with a keen eye you can usually spot some things that are off about the email. If you look carefully at the sender email there might be something misspelled or the link in the email will be different that the normal url you would use to access your bank's website. In the case of credential harvesters there are usually some red flags on the website as well such as an unusual logo. A lot of people might not think twice about taking action when it comes to securing their bank account which is why it's important that current cyber security awareness programs focus more on facilitating long term behavioral changes as opposed to short term learning objectives (Althobaiti & Alsufyani, 2024).

Protection Methods

Authentication and password security

Although multi-factor authentication significantly enhances the security of your personal information,these systems can become less effective due to common bad practices such as weak passwords and password reuse.

Because Multi-factor authentication can lengthen the time it takes for you to access your applications many people see this lack of usability as an inconvenience and this plays a significant role in determining whether stronger authentication methods are widely adopted (Reese et al., 2019).

Core Concepts

Why stronger authentication matters

Authentication systems, particularly multi-factor authentication, are crucial when it comes to protecting a user's sensitive information.

Multi-factor authentication Weak passwords Password reuse
Authentication Methods

Different systems, different tradeoffs

Recent research into various authentication systems highlight token based, password based and biometric authentication like face id or finger print each with varying levels of effectiveness and usability (Attacks and Defenses in User Authentication Systems, 2021). Some of the newer authentication methods like biometric scans are not commonplace due to financial and potential usability concerns despite being better options than just a password based system.

Password Managers

Useful, but dependent on adoption

For password only based authentication systems password managers are sometimes used to increase security by generating long complex passwords for you and saving them in a database so you can easily retrieve them without the need to remember the entire password yourself. Password Managers while secure their effectiveness depends on user adoption. Many people will avoid using password managers due to perceived complexity/inconvenience and a lack of understanding in regards to how they work.(Pearman et al., 2019).

Education

Interactive cybersecurity awareness

Traditional methods of educating users on cybersecurity and how important it is to protect their data can be inefficient in successfully modifying user behavior.

Recent research further solidifies this gradual change to more interactive earring methods being beneficial in promoting long term behavioural changes when it comes to being more conscientious about how users protect their data (Althobaiti & Alsufyani, 2024).

Interactive tools will allow users to be more engaged with the content whether it be via video, quizzes or simulations, these methods are more effective in changing long term behaviour as knowledge alone does not always translate into action (Öner, Çetin, & Savas, 2025).
