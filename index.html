The Human Element

The Human
Element

A fact that may surprise you is human behaviour is one of the primary drivers of cybersecurity threats. Susceptibility is not only tied to gaps in technical knowledge but also behaviour and psychological factors such as risk perception, prior experiences and motivation (Öner, Çetin, & Savas, 2025). For example, you might prioritize your time and convenience over security if you believe the process involved in protecting your information to be cumbersome. This tendency contributes to bad practices such as weak password creation or disregarding security warnings. Improving your understanding of how cybersecurity ties into your everyday life can go a long way in protecting yourself on the internet.

Social Engineering

Social Engineering

Social engineering is a common tactic used by attackers to take advantage of the human element and manipulate others by leveraging trust, urgency and authority to gain access to sensitive information or restricted areas. It's natural for us to want to be helpful especially when talking to someone that seems to be in need or distress, but its important to exercise caution. Because social engineering can be used to bypass even the most modern technical defenses, it is considered an evolving threat.

Phishing is one of the most common forms of social engineering and its designed around coercing people into giving up sensitive information or installing viruses onto your computer such as ransomware. An example of a phishing attempt would be receiving an email that appears to be legitimate from somewhere like a bank asking you to click on a link to secure our account due to an “unauthorized purchase”. In reality there is no unauthorized charge on your account and the attacker will use that link to either install malware on your system or harvest your credentials by creating a fake webpage that looks somewhat similar to the one you might be used to using for your bank and wait for you to enter your login credentials. Thankfully with a keen eye you can usually spot some things that are off about the email. If you look carefully at the sender email there might be something misspelled or the link in the email will be different that the normal url you would use to access your bank's website. In the case of credential harvesters there are usually some red flags on the website as well such as an unusual logo. A lot of people might not think twice about taking action when it comes to securing their bank account which is why it's important that current cyber security awareness programs focus more on facilitating long term behavioral changes as opposed to short term learning objectives (Althobaiti & Alsufyani, 2024).

Authentication & Password Security

Authentication & Password Security

Authentication systems, particularly multi-factor authentication, are crucial when it comes to protecting a user's sensitive information. Although multi-factor authentication significantly enhances the security of your personal information,these systems can become less effective due to common bad practices such as weak passwords and password reuse. Because Multi-factor authentication can lengthen the time it takes for you to access your applications many people see this lack of usability as an inconvenience and this plays a significant role in determining whether stronger authentication methods are widely adopted (Reese et al., 2019).

Recent research into various authentication systems highlight token based, password based and biometric authentication like face id or finger print each with varying levels of effectiveness and usability (Attacks and Defenses in User Authentication Systems, 2021). Some of the newer authentication methods like biometric scans are not commonplace due to financial and potential usability concerns despite being better options than just a password based system.

For password only based authentication systems password managers are sometimes used to increase security by generating long complex passwords for you and saving them in a database so you can easily retrieve them without the need to remember the entire password yourself. Password Managers while secure their effectiveness depends on user adoption. Many people will avoid using password managers due to perceived complexity/inconvenience and a lack of understanding in regards to how they work.(Pearman et al., 2019).

Security vs. Usability

Balancing Security and Usability

One of the Major hurdles in cybersecurity is finding a balance between strong security and ease of use. Research highlights that when given an opportunity to choose an authentication method many people chose the most convenient options even if it compromises the security of their data (Reese et al., 2019).

Interactive Cybersecurity Awareness

Interactive Cybersecurity Awareness

Traditional methods of educating users on cybersecurity and how important it is to protect their data can be inefficient in successfully modifying user behavior. Recent research further solidifies this gradual change to more interactive earring methods being beneficial in promoting long term behavioural changes when it comes to being more conscientious about how users protect their data (Althobaiti & Alsufyani, 2024).

Interactive tools will allow users to be more engaged with the content whether it be via video, quizzes or simulations, these methods are more effective in changing long term behaviour as knowledge alone does not always translate into action (Öner, Çetin, & Savas, 2025).
